Discover Europe's flower visitors and pollinators

Explore the relationships between plants and the animals that visit their flowers across Europe.

Bees, butterflies, hoverflies, moths and many other animals visit flowers, often transferring pollen between them. Understanding these relationships helps us protect biodiversity and the natural processes that support wild plants and crops.

EuroAPPA (the European Atlas of Plant–Pollinator Associations) brings together scientific records of these interactions, making them easier to explore and use.

Where does this information come from?

EuroAPPA brings together existing information about flower-visiting animals and the plants they interact with. These records come from scientific studies and other biodiversity datasets. We organise the records by geographical region and use recognised scientific names to make the information easier to compare.

The information can help researchers, conservationists and other users understand which flower-visiting insects have been recorded in different areas of Europe.

Important: The lists reflect currently available records, rather than every insect that occurs in a region. Some areas have been studied much more thoroughly than others, and not every insect visiting a flower necessarily pollinates it.
